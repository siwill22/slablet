# Plan: show the mantle field as S40RTS would see it

Handoff plan, 11 November 2026. Stage 0 (removing the generic blur) is done but not committed; nothing else has been
built. Start at Stage 1. Each stage ends at a gate: figures, a short README, then wait for Simon.

## 1. Why

Until 11 November slablet blurred its dVs field with a spherical Gaussian (σ = 5° horizontally, 150 km in depth) and
described the result as what tomography would see. The blur dates from the first mantle code (PlateDriver commit
`67f6585`, May 2026), where it equalled the 5° grid spacing. The sweeps of 31 October found that wider blurs scored
better, and `configs/muller2022_tuned.yaml` attributes that to hiding higher degrees. The 2 November sharp-slab work
kept 5° for scoring only.

The blur removed far more structure than tomography loses. At present day in `muller2022_refined`, the blurred field
kept 2–14 % of its variance above spherical-harmonic degree 8 (features narrower than about 22°) at 1000–2500 km,
against 18–70 % in four tomography models (Table 1), and almost nothing above degree 16, where those models keep
5–45 %. Even before the blur, the model had no more fine structure than these models at any depth, so the blur took it
further from all of them. The blur hardly changed the scores. The degree 1–8 correlation with REVEAL was 0.48 with
the blur and 0.47 without it, and with SEMUCB-WM1 0.58 and 0.55. The overlaps of the slowest 30 % and fastest 20 %
moved by 0.01 or less, and the OPT1 scores by 0.02 or less in the lower mantle.

Table 1. Share of variance in degrees 9–40 at present day (`muller2022_refined`; tomography as distributed in the
Schouten et al. supplementary models).

| Depth | Model, blurred | Model, unblurred | REVEAL | SEMUCB-WM1 | GLAD-M25 (vsv) | GAP-P4 (P) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1000 km | 14 % | 45 % | 60 % | 70 % | 59 % | 43 % |
| 2000 km | 5 % | 23 % | 44 % | 36 % | 38 % | 30 % |
| 2500 km | 2 % | 8 % | 35 % | 30 % | 25 % | 18 % |

Tomographic smearing is not a uniform Gaussian. It follows ray paths, depends on where earthquakes and stations are,
mostly lowers amplitudes and smears structure in depth, and leaves sharp features where coverage is dense. Ritsema et
al. (2007) proposed passing a geodynamic model through the resolution operator R of one tomographic inversion and
comparing the result with that tomographic model. Schuberth et al. (2009) used the same approach with S20RTS to ask
whether temperature alone explains the seismic heterogeneity.

Here we plan to replace the blur with the resolution operator of S40RTS (Ritsema et al. 2011). The questions are:
Does the filtered model match S40RTS in pattern, and how far apart are their amplitudes? Which model features (slab
curtains, pile edges, plume conduits) survive the filter, and where are they smeared? Does a like-with-like comparison
change any earlier conclusion?

## 2. The filter

R works in S40RTS's own parameterisation: spherical harmonics to degree 40 in each layer and 21 cubic splines in
depth. The steps are: (1) expand each of the model's 29 layers (50–2850 km every 100 km, 1° grid, dVs in %) to degree
40; (2) project the stack onto the 21 radial splines; (3) multiply by R, built from the eigenvectors of the inversion
(`S40RTS.evc`, 4.5 GB) at the published damping; (4) evaluate the result at the model's depths. Steps 1 and 2 already
remove structure that S40RTS's parameterisation cannot hold. Step 3 removes what its data and damping cannot resolve.

We will use `srts` (`pip install srts`, MIT licence), G-ADOPT's Python port of Ritsema and Koelemeijer's Fortran
codes. Its README reports agreement with the Fortran to 6–7 significant digits. On first use it downloads the S12RTS,
S20RTS and S40RTS operators (about 4.8 GB). Its dependencies (NumPy, SciPy, pyshtools 4.13, h5py 3.13) are already in
`pygmt17`. The Fortran codes and operator files are also on Koelemeijer's download page and on Zenodo, which gives an
independent check if `srts` gives anything unexpected.

Every step works on the sphere (harmonic expansion and radial splines), and none assumes a flat surface. The 0 Ma field
is in the optimised mantle frame of Müller et al. (2022), anchored to plate 0, so its coordinates are present-day
geographic longitude and latitude. Any difference between geodetic and geocentric latitude (at most 0.19°) is small
compared with the 4.5° half-wavelength of degree 40. The field has zero mean on each layer, as S40RTS does.

R describes the data coverage behind today's S40RTS. Applied to the 0 Ma field, it gives a testable comparison.
Applied to a past field, it shows how today's S40RTS would see a mantle that looked like that. This can help when
reading the viewer, but there is nothing to compare it with.

## 3. Stages

**Stage 1: install and check the operator.** Install `srts` into `pygmt17` and run its tests. Then make two checks.
First, evaluate `srts`'s own S40RTS model at 600, 1000, 2000 and 2800 km and compare it with the grids in
`~/Data/SeismicTomography/s40rts/`, which tests sign, units, longitude convention and depth. The two should agree to
the precision of the grids. Second, compute point-spread functions: filter a single blob (σ = 2°, 200 km thick, +1 %)
placed in turn under the western Pacific at 1000 km, the southern Indian Ocean at 1000 km and Africa at 2700 km, and
map the output. Gate: maps of both checks, the peak amplitude kept and the area each blob spreads over, and the time
and memory for one filtering.

**Stage 2: filter the present-day reference run.** Filter `muller2022_refined` at 0 Ma. Gate: the map sequence at 600,
1000, 1500, 2000, 2500 and 2800 km with the model, the filtered model and S40RTS side by side. Show each depth twice,
once on one shared colour scale and once scaled to each panel's rms, so that pattern can be read separately from
amplitude. Then add degree spectra and rms by depth for all three.

Amplitudes already differ before filtering: rms 1.2–5.0 % in the model against 0.4–0.8 % in S40RTS over 1000–2800
km, with slab peaks of 10–27 % (Table 2). The filter will lower the model's amplitudes. Any difference that remains is
a property of the model to report, not a target to tune towards. If it is large, name the ingredient responsible.
Candidates are slablets stacking where they rest on the core–mantle boundary, the pile's −5 %, and the conversion of
−500 K at −0.001 % per K.

**Stage 3: make it part of a run.** Add an optional step, off by default so that the plain run stays unchanged and no
slower. It writes the filtered field beside each `mantle_temp` file as `mantle_s40rts_<age>Ma.nc` (variable
`dVs_s40rts`), at the ages chosen in section 4. Add S40RTS to the configs' `tomography:` list, scored against the
filtered field. REVEAL and SEMUCB-WM1 stay scored against `dVs` at degrees 1–8, because R belongs to S40RTS alone. The
Earth viewer gains a layer, "Shear-velocity anomaly as S40RTS would see it", in place of the removed `dvs_tomo`. Filter
`muller2022_refined`, `clennett2020_refined` and `muller2019_refined`. Gate: the viewer and the new scores beside the
old ones.

**Stage 4 (only if Stage 2 or 3 ranks runs differently from the degree 1–8 scores).** Re-check the conclusions that
rest on tomography scores, in particular the reference-frame and stall tests of early November.

## 4. Choices for Simon

1. Ages. Filter only 0 Ma (for scoring and a present-day viewer layer), or every output age as well, labelled as
   today's S40RTS view? Proposed: every age if Stage 1 shows one filtering takes seconds, with scores at 0 Ma only.
2. Amplitude. Keep the S40RTS score scale-free (correlation and overlap, as now) and report rms beside it, or add an
   amplitude term? Proposed: scale-free, with rms reported.
3. Other operators. `srts` also has S20RTS and S12RTS, and `llnltofi` (G-ADOPT) has the LLNL-G3D-JPS operator, which
   is built from P-wave data. Proposed: S40RTS alone until Stage 3 has passed its gate.

## 5. Stage 0, done 11 November 2026 (not committed)

In slablet (branch `asthenosphere-layer`) and PlateDriver (branch `mantle-extract`):

- `tomo_smooth_horiz_deg`, `tomo_smooth_depth_km` and `dvs_save_native` are gone from `MantleParams`. `dVs` is the
  model's field, and its parts are `dVs__<source>` (`dvs_save_components`).
- `slablet.current_dvs` reads runs written before the change: `dVs_native` becomes `dVs` and the blurred field is
  dropped. Runs that never saved `dVs_native` (e.g. `runs_real/muller2022`) hold only the blurred field.
- Readers use it: `dyntopo`, `geode.export_run`, `tomography_score`, `compare_opt1`, `sweep_resolution`,
  `viewer/prep/export.py`, `viewer/prep/mantle_5myr.py`, `scripts/check_against_hc.py`. `sweep_opt1` no longer writes
  `compare_opt1_native.csv`. The viewer's `dvs_tomo` layer is gone, and the viewer data need re-exporting.
- Every config in `configs/` has the keys removed. `configs/sweeps/` is left as the record of what was run, so
  re-running a sweep that sets the blur stops with "unknown mantle parameters". PlateDriver's `config.yaml`,
  `configs/current_model.yaml` and README no longer mention the blur (its `MantleParams.from_config` ignores unknown
  keys, so older PlateDriver configs run without the blur).
- The new `dVs` is identical to the old `dVs_native` in three set-ups (10° grid; with pile and parts; 1° grid with
  the slab width profile). The golden test applies the old blur inside the test and still reproduces the
  pre-extraction output exactly. All 151 tests pass.
